<section class="section" id="weekend">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="section-head">
      <div>
        <span class="tag">RACE WEEKEND</span>
        <h2>Three days, three jobs</h2>
      </div>
      <p>A Grand Prix weekend is built to get a car from an unknown setup on Friday to its fastest, most reliable version by Sunday afternoon.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="weekend">
      <div class="wk-card">
        <div class="session">FRIDAY — SATURDAY AM</div>
        <h3>Practice</h3>
        <p>Three sessions where teams test tyre choices, fuel loads, and car balance. Times don't count for anything except information.</p>
        <div class="duration">3 × 60min</div>
      </div>
      <div class="wk-card">
        <div class="session">SATURDAY</div>
        <h3>Qualifying</h3>
        <p>A knockout format across three stages sets Sunday's starting grid — the slowest cars are eliminated after each stage.</p>
        <div class="duration">Q1 / Q2 / Q3</div>
      </div>
      <div class="wk-card">
        <div class="session">SUNDAY</div>
        <h3>The Race</h3>
        <p>A fixed distance (roughly 305km) run over as many laps as that takes, with pit stops for fresh tyres built into the strategy.</p>
        <div class="duration">~90–120min</div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section" id="car" style="padding-top:0;">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="section-head">
      <div>
        <span class="tag">THE CAR</span>
        <h2>What's actually under that bodywork</h2>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="anatomy">
      <div class="anatomy-photo">
        <img src="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/2006_McLaren_MP4-21_Formula_1_race_car_(5963356958).jpg" alt="A Formula One race car on display, showing its bodywork and wings">
      </div>
      <div class="anatomy-list">
        <div class="a-item">
          <div class="tag-n">01</div>
          <div><h4>Front and rear wings</h4><p>Shaped like upside-down aircraft wings, they push the car down onto the track instead of lifting it up.</p></div>
        </div>
        <div class="a-item">
          <div class="tag-n">02</div>
          <div><h4>The monocoque</h4><p>A carbon-fibre survival cell around the driver, built to absorb enormous impact forces in a crash.</p></div>
        </div>
        <div class="a-item">
          <div class="tag-n">03</div>
          <div><h4>Hybrid power unit</h4><p>A turbocharged engine paired with energy-recovery systems that harvest braking and exhaust heat for extra power.</p></div>
        </div>
        <div class="a-item">
          <div class="tag-n">04</div>
          <div><h4>18-inch slick tyres</h4><p>No tread pattern in the dry — maximum rubber touching the track for grip, with separate wet-weather tyres for rain.</p></div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section" id="pit" style="padding-top:0;">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="section-head">
      <div>
        <span class="tag">PIT STOPS</span>
        <h2>Twenty people, two seconds</h2>
      </div>
      <p>A modern pit stop is one of the most rehearsed few seconds in sport — every crew member has exactly one job.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="pitstop">
      <div class="pit-photo">
        <img src="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Honda_pit_stop_Malaysia_2008.jpg" alt="A Formula One pit crew changing tyres during a pit stop">
      </div>
      <div class="pit-steps">
        <div class="p-step"><div class="clock">0.0s</div><div><h4>Car arrives at its marked box</h4><p>The driver brakes to a precise stop within centimetres of the pit crew's position.</p></div></div>
        <div class="p-step"><div class="clock">0.3s</div><div><h4>Jacks lift front and rear</h4><p>Two jack operators raise the car off the ground almost simultaneously.</p></div></div>
        <div class="p-step"><div class="clock">0.8s</div><div><h4>Wheels off, wheels on</h4><p>Three-person crews per corner remove one wheel nut, pull the old tyre, and fit the new one.</p></div></div>
        <div class="p-step"><div class="clock">~2.2s</div><div><h4>Car is released</h4><p>The lollipop or light system signals it's safe to rejoin — a fraction of a second early or late can cost a position.</p></div></div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section cockpit">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="section-head">
      <div>
        <span class="tag">COCKPIT</span>
        <h2>A steering wheel that does more than steer</h2>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="cockpit-grid">
      <div class="cockpit-photo">
        <img src="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Alpine_F1_steering_wheel.jpg" alt="A close-up of a Formula One steering wheel, covered in buttons and dials">
      </div>
      <div class="cockpit-text">
        <p>The steering wheel is closer to a cockpit control panel than a road car's. Drivers adjust brake balance, engine mapping, differential settings, and radio channels — all without taking their hands off it.</p>
        <p>A small display in the centre shows lap times, tyre temperatures, fuel remaining, and warnings, all read at speeds well over 300km/h.</p>
        <div class="cockpit-facts">
          <div><strong>20+</strong><span>Switches and dials on a typical wheel</span></div>
          <div><strong>~1.3kg</strong><span>Approximate wheel weight</span></div>
          <div><strong>&gt;300</strong><span>Km/h reached on the longest straights</span></div>
          <div><strong>~50</strong><span>G-force endured under heavy braking</span></div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section" id="circuits">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="section-head">
      <div>
        <span class="tag">CIRCUITS</span>
        <h2>Not every track is built the same way</h2>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="circuits">
      <div class="circuit-photo">
        <img src="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Monza_aerial_photo.jpg" alt="Aerial photograph of the Autodromo Nazionale Monza racing circuit">
      </div>
      <div class="circuit-info">
        <h3>Two broad categories</h3>
        <div class="circuit-type">
          <div class="name">PERMANENT CIRCUIT</div>
          <p>Purpose-built tracks like Monza, shown here, used year-round for racing and testing, with run-off areas designed for high-speed corners.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="circuit-type">
          <div class="name">STREET CIRCUIT</div>
          <p>Public roads closed off for the weekend, lined by walls instead of gravel — tighter, less forgiving, and often slower on average.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="circuit-type">
          <div class="name">HYBRID CIRCUIT</div>
          <p>A mix of dedicated racing sections and public roads, combining higher speeds with some street-circuit-style tight corners.</p>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section" id="flags" style="padding-top:0;">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="section-head">
      <div>
        <span class="tag">FLAGS &amp; SIGNALS</span>
        <h2>How marshals talk to drivers at 300km/h</h2>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="flags">
      <div class="flag-item">
        <div class="flag-swatch" style="background:#1a8f3c;"></div>
        <div><h4>Green flag</h4><p>Track is clear — session or race is starting, or a hazard has just been cleared.</p></div>
      </div>
      <div class="flag-item">
        <div class="flag-swatch" style="background:#e0c200;"></div>
        <div><h4>Yellow flag</h4><p>Danger ahead — no overtaking in that sector until the hazard is passed.</p></div>
      </div>
      <div class="flag-item">
        <div class="flag-swatch" style="background:#cc1f1f;"></div>
        <div><h4>Red flag</h4><p>Session stopped entirely, usually for a serious incident or unsafe conditions.</p></div>
      </div>
      <div class="flag-item">
        <div class="flag-swatch" style="background:linear-gradient(90deg, #111 50%, #eee 50%);"></div>
        <div><h4>Chequered flag</h4><p>The session or race has finished — shown to every car as it crosses the line.</p></div>
      </div>
      <div class="flag-item">
        <div class="flag-swatch" style="background:#1657c4;"></div>
        <div><h4>Blue flag</h4><p>Shown to a car about to be lapped, asking it to let the faster car through.</p></div>
      </div>
      <div class="flag-item">
        <div class="flag-swatch" style="background:repeating-linear-gradient(45deg,#111,#111 4px,#e0c200 4px,#e0c200 8px);"></div>
        <div><h4>Yellow and red stripes</h4><p>Warns of a slippery track surface, often oil or debris rather than a stopped car.</p></div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section" id="history" style="padding-top:0;">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="section-head">
      <div>
        <span class="tag">MILESTONES</span>
        <h2>A short history of the sport</h2>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="timeline">
      <div class="t-item">
        <div class="yr">1950</div>
        <h4>The first World Championship season</h4>
        <p>The inaugural Formula One World Championship season began at Silverstone in the United Kingdom.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="t-item">
        <div class="yr">1977</div>
        <h4>Turbocharged engines arrive</h4>
        <p>Renault introduced the first turbocharged engine in the modern era, changing engine design for the following decade.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="t-item">
        <div class="yr">1994</div>
        <h4>A wave of major safety reforms</h4>
        <p>Following serious accidents that season, the sport introduced sweeping changes to car design and circuit safety standards.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="t-item">
        <div class="yr">2014</div>
        <h4>The hybrid power unit era begins</h4>
        <p>New regulations introduced turbo-hybrid engines with energy recovery systems, reshaping both performance and strategy.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="t-item">
        <div class="yr">2022</div>
        <h4>A return to ground-effect aerodynamics</h4>
        <p>Major rule changes brought back underbody aerodynamics last common in the 1970s and 80s, aimed at improving close racing.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
